# Steepest Descent
I've been reading Muon recently, mainly based on [ref1](https://spaces.ac.cn/archives/10739). When training LLM, we want to have two properties

1. Minimize loss (steepest descent)
2. Small update on the params (stability)

The first property is to make sure the training converges fast while the second property is to make the training stable. We can turn this into an optimization problem

$$
\begin{align}
\min_{\Delta\Theta} & & L(\Theta+\Delta\Theta) \\
s.t. & & \rho(\Delta\Theta) < \eta\\
\end{align}
$$

We would like to find $\Delta\Theta$ to minimize our loss such that we don't change the model $\Theta$ too much.

Assuming $\Delta\Theta$ is very small, we can expand $L(\Theta+\Delta\Theta)$ at $\Theta$ using its first-order Taylor expansion

$$
L(\Theta+\Delta\Theta) \approx L(\Theta) + Tr(G^{T}\Delta\Theta)
$$

where $G^{T}=\nabla L(\Theta)$. Now we can rewrite the optimization problem as follows

$$
\begin{align}
\min_{\Delta\Theta} & & Tr(G^{T}\Delta\Theta) \\
s.t. & & \rho(\Delta\Theta) < \eta\\
\end{align}
$$

The next question is how to select $\rho$. 

## SGD
If we use Frobenius norm (essentially, we flatten matrix into a vector), then the optimization problem becomes

$$
\begin{align}
\min_{\Delta\Theta} & & Tr(G^{T}\Delta\Theta) \\
s.t. & & ||\Delta\Theta||_{F} < \eta\\
\end{align}
$$

The solution to it is SGD, namely $\Delta\Theta = -\eta G$. In other words, SGD is the steepest descent using Frobenius norm as a measure of divergence.

## Muon
The issue of using Frobenius norm is that we flatten matrix into a vector and ignores the geometric shape of the matrix. Now consider spectral norm instead of Frobenius norm, we have following opimitzation problem

$$
\begin{align}
\min_{\Delta\Theta} & & Tr(G^{T}\Delta\Theta) \\
s.t. & & ||\Delta\Theta||_{2} < \eta\\
\end{align}
$$

The solution to it is Muon, namely $\Delta\Theta = -\eta \space msign(G)$. In other words, Muon is the steepest descent using spectral norm as a measure of divergence.

# Steepest Ascent

This reminds me of TRPO where we are dealing with a similar optimization problem

$$
\begin{align}
\max_{\Theta} & & \mathbb{E}_{s,a\sim\pi_{\Theta_{old}}}[\frac{\pi_{\Theta}(s, a)}{\pi_{\Theta_{old}}(s, a)}A(s, a)] \\
s.t. & & \mathbb{E}_{s\sim\pi_{\Theta_{old}}}[D_{KL}(\pi_{\Theta_{old}}(\cdot|s)||\pi_{\Theta}(\cdot|s))] < \delta
\end{align}
$$

Conceptually, This is similar to the derivation of SGD and Muon. We want to maximize the advantage (steepest ascent) such that we don't change the policy model too much (stability). Here instead of measuring divergence in parameter space, we are measuring the divergence between two probability distributions.

To solve the problem, we also assume $\Delta\Theta$ is small to linearlize the objective.

$$
\begin{align}
\max_{\Delta\Theta} & & Tr(G^{T}\Delta\Theta) \\
s.t. & & \rho(\pi_{\Theta}, \pi_{\Theta+\Delta\Theta}) < \delta
\end{align}
$$
Where $\rho$ measures the divergence of two probability distributions.

# Observation
* In SGD and Muon, the stability is measured in parameter space. This makes sense becasue the loss is cross entropy. Intuitievely what we do here is to get as close as possible to target distribution while keeps the parameters stable. Here we explicitly require the parameter updates to be small.
* In TRPO, the stability is measured by KL divergence. This also makes sense because we are maximiaze the expected advantage while keeps the output distribution stable. Here we explicitly require the distribution updates to be small. In other words, we might see big parameter updates as long as the output distribution is small.
* We can abstract the problem both for pre-training and post-training as following
   * We want to minimize the loss via steepest descent or maximize the reward via steepest ascent.
   * We want stability when updating the model.
